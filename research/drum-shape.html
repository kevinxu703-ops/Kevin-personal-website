Spectral geometry · Academic work 01

Can One Hear the Shape of a Drum?

A boundary-operator approach to the vibration frequencies of analytic planar drums, tested through forward spectral calculations and a restricted coefficient-fitting experiment for the Neumann oval.

Author
Jiankun Xu
Supervision
Jian Deng · Lingyun Chang
Focus
Analytic planar domains
Document
Research report · 2026

Abstract

The question of whether the Dirichlet spectrum determines the shape of a drum is a central problem in inverse spectral geometry. For a bounded simply connected planar domain Ω ⊂ ℝ² with real-analytic Jordan boundary, we construct an entire family of boundary operators W(λ), called the Wronskian operators. Its kernel is linearly isomorphic to the corresponding Dirichlet eigenspace, and its regularized determinant vanishes at the Dirichlet eigenvalues. In conformal Fourier coordinates, we derive a representation of W(λ) from the coefficients of the normalized Riemann map. We illustrate the finite construction through a forward spectral calculation and a restricted three-parameter fitting experiment for the Neumann oval with R = 1.8.

Selected findings

From an operator to a finite computation

Forward calculation

Seven spectral candidates.

Finite matrices are built from Riemann-map coefficients. For the Neumann oval, seven low-frequency candidates stabilize across the tested truncation levels and approach independent conformal Galerkin estimates.

Restricted inverse test

Three coefficients, five starting points.

Varying only the first three nonzero coefficients, four of five tested initializations meet the residual criterion. The spectral targets come from the same truncated forward model.

Scope: the report establishes a spectral criterion and a finite computational construction. It does not establish general uniqueness or stability of shape recovery, or certified convergence of computed eigenvalues.
